---
title: How to price a gaming café: hourly rates, packages and happy hours
date: 2026-10-01
description: A practical way to set hourly rates, prepaid packages and off-peak offers that fill quiet hours without giving away your busy ones.
---

Most gaming cafés start with one hourly rate and add discounts whenever things feel slow. A year later nobody can say what an hour actually costs. Here's a simpler way to build prices that stay understandable for players and staff.

## Start from your busy hours

Your evenings and weekends sell themselves. Price them first, at a rate that covers rent, power, staff and the cost of replacing hardware every few years, and leaves a margin. Every other price is a discount from this one, so get it right first.

Look at what the cafés near you charge, but don't just copy the cheapest: a better chair, a faster PC or a quieter room are reasons to charge more, and players will pay for them.

## Charge more for better rooms, not more for everything

Different areas deserve different prices: standard PCs, a VIP room, consoles, VR. Keep it to a few areas with one clear rate each. Players should be able to read your price list in ten seconds.

## Sell time in advance

Prepaid packages — "5 hours for the price of 4" — get money in before the player sits down and bring them back. Two or three packages are enough. Give packages an expiry date so they don't sit on your books forever.

## Fill the quiet hours on purpose

Weekday mornings and early afternoons are empty in most venues. A happy hour (a lower rate or bonus minutes between, say, 10:00 and 15:00 on weekdays) turns empty seats into some income. Keep it to the hours that are really quiet — if your happy hour fills, it's eating into full-price play.

Other off-peak ideas that work:

- **Student days** — a lower rate with a student card on one or two weekdays.
- **Birthday bonus** — free time in the week of a member's birthday brings groups.
- **First visit** — a small bonus to get a new player to come back once.

## Don't stack everything

When several offers apply at once, decide which one wins. The usual rule: the single best discount applies, and only offers you mark as stackable add on top. Cap the total, so no combination can make an hour free by accident.
