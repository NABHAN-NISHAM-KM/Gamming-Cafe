A tournament fills the venue on a night that would otherwise be quiet, and turns players who come alone into a community. It also goes wrong quickly if the bracket lives on a whiteboard. Here's a routine that works.

## Pick the right format

- **Single elimination** is quick and easy to follow. Good for 8–32 players on one evening.
- **Double elimination** gives everyone a second chance, and takes longer.
- **Round robin** — everyone plays everyone. Fair, but only for small groups.
- **Swiss** works well for larger groups: a few rounds where players with the same record meet, no one is knocked out early.
- **League** runs over several weeks and brings the same players back every week.

For a first event, single elimination with 16 players is hard to beat.

## Set the entry fee and prize honestly

Decide before you open sign-ups: the entry fee, what the prize is, and how ties are split. If the prize comes from entry fees, say so. Free-entry events with a small prize (free hours on the winner's account) are a cheap way to start.

## Take entries ahead of time

Open sign-ups a week before. Taking the entry fee at sign-up means fewer no-shows. Enforce the team size, and make sure one player can't join two teams.

## Check in, then seed

Ask players to check in 30 minutes before the start. Close entries, then seed the bracket — spread the strongest players apart so they don't meet in round one. With an odd number of players, the top seeds get byes.

## Run it from one screen

Staff should report each result once, and the bracket should advance on its own. Assign stations to matches so players know where to sit. Put the bracket on a screen people can see.

## Finish properly

Announce placements, pay the prizes on the night, and thank everyone. Points or a small reward for every player, not just the winner, bring people back for the next one.
